Africa & India: who could fund the corridors.

Potential partners for TechBBQ's Nordic–Africa and Nordic–India summits, placed on the stakeholder map and checked against real sources.

How I picked them.

Each candidate had to pass three questions. The goal is to move each one into the "manage closely" corner of the stakeholder map: partners who pay and help shape the programme.

  1. Does it already spend money or time on this region? A budget that exists is easier to win than one that has to be created.
  2. Does it fit the programme's focus? The Novo Nordisk Foundation grant targets life science, deep tech and AI, and climate and agri-food.
  3. Is it new, or an existing partner who could grow?
TechBBQ's 2025 Nordic–Africa impact report names early-stage capital as the region's main gap, and asks development finance institutions (DFIs) and foundations for catalytic capital. That's why the Africa list leans on DFIs and funds. (report)

Lessons from Life Science Without Borders.

TechBBQ's life science programme is a ready template. The same foundation funded it for three years (2023–2025), and it grew from Danish to Nordic to international. The new Africa and India grant is also three years.

Where I'd start.

  1. A Nordic DFI Investor Room with Norfund and Swedfund

    The Africa report's biggest gap is early-stage capital. Both institutions have the budget and a track record in African tech funds, so this answers the report's own recommendation.

  2. ICDK Africa as co-organiser of the Africa summit

    The quickest win. ICDK is already a partner and already runs this model in India. Repeating what works beats inventing something new.

  3. Open the India summit to all five Nordic countries

    The May 2026 India–Nordic partnership is a political window. Shared funding from Sweden, Norway and Finland grows the summit and cuts reliance on a single grant.
