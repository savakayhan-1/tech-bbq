A new corridor: Nordic–Türkiye.

Türkiye already pays to send startup delegations to Europe's big tech events, but I found no Turkish delegation at TechBBQ. That makes it a ready-made corridor for TechBBQ 2027 (8–9 September, Bella Center).

Why Türkiye, why now.

  • TechBBQ has a proven corridor model. The Nordic–India summit (since 2024) and the Nordic–Africa summit already run inside the main event, and India's DPIIT is a Pioneer partner.
  • Türkiye has the budget and the habit. Under the Ministry of Industry and Technology's call for global entrepreneurship events, development agencies take 100+ Turkish startups a year to Slush, Web Summit, CES and Web Summit Qatar. (BEBKA) Invest in Türkiye runs pavilions at VivaTech and Web Summit, and HİB led the largest delegation in London Tech Week history in 2026: around 60 companies and 250+ delegates. (Startups Magazine)
  • TechBBQ is missing from that calendar. Slush shows Turkish startups already want the Nordics. TechBBQ is the natural second Nordic stop, two months earlier.
  • I can open this door. I'm Turkish, worked in Izmir's and Istanbul's events and culture scene for years, and know how public funders write calls and judge proposals.

Flagship pitch: the Nordic–Türkiye Startup Bridge.

A half-day programme inside TechBBQ 2027, built on the Nordic–India model, that gives Turkish funders one place to show their startups to Nordic investors and corporates.

  1. Türkiye pavilion

    15–20 selected startups in the main hall: AI, fintech, gaming, climate, health.

  2. Bridge session

    Turkish and Nordic VCs on a TechBBQ stage, talking cross-border investment.

  3. Grill sessions and matchmaking

    Each startup pitches to a curated investor group, with 1:1 meetings pre-booked in the event app.

  4. Diplomatic Soirée

    An investor dinner co-hosted with the Embassy of Türkiye.

Suggested package: Conqueror pavilion (€37,000 at 2025 prices) for the lead funder, plus Explorer or Core add-ons for co-funders such as technoparks.

Timeline: first meetings in Q1 2027, contracts by April, startup selection in May–June, summit on 8–9 September, ROI report in October.

A two-way bridge: mobility for founders.

The corridor isn't only about one event. Denmark and Türkiye already have legal and visa routes that let founders move in both directions, and a TechBBQ corridor can make them visible.

RouteWhat it isWhy it matters for the corridor
Ankara Agreement (EEC–Türkiye Association Agreement, 1963)Gives Turkish nationals special rights in Denmark, and aims to strengthen economic and trade links. Its standstill clause means rules for economically active Turkish citizens can't be stricter than in 1980.A long-standing legal basis for Turkish companies doing business in Denmark, and a strong talking point for Turkish funders.
Visa exemption for service providersTurkish citizens living and working in Türkiye can enter Denmark without a visa to provide a short service, under 90 days. It doesn't cover tourism or family visits.Useful for some company staff, but not for delegates simply attending TechBBQ: they still need a visa. Delegation planning must start early.
Start-up Denmark (Türkiye → Denmark)A business plan is approved by an independent expert panel set up by the Danish Business Authority, then the founder applies to SIRI for a permit. Annual quota of 75.The next step for Turkish startups who meet Nordic investors at TechBBQ and want to stay.
Türkiye Tech Visa (Nordics → Türkiye)Launched in September 2024: a three-year work permit plus support for foreign tech startups. It drew applications from 47 countries, and 22 startups were approved to relocate. (Hürriyet Daily News, AA)Gives Turkish partners something to offer Nordic founders, so the corridor works in both directions.

Session idea: a Market entry clinic

A 45-minute session at TechBBQ 2027 where Turkish founders hear about Start-up Denmark, and Nordic founders hear about the Türkiye Tech Visa and Turcorn, followed by 1:1 office hours.

  • It connects an existing partner. The Danish Business Authority, which sets up the Start-up Denmark panel, is already a TechBBQ Core partner.
  • It strengthens the Turcorn pitch. Türkiye's Ministry of Industry and Technology already promotes the Tech Visa and Turcorn at international events, so a Copenhagen session fits what they already do.
  • It gives delegations a reason to return. Founders leave with a concrete next step, not just contacts.

This is a summary for partnership planning, not legal or immigration advice. Rules and processing times change; check nyidanmark.dk and the official Tech Visa site before any delegation plan.

The same playbook, more countries.

Türkiye isn't the only gap. At least six more countries already pay for national pavilions at Slush but aren't on TechBBQ's partner list. Combined potential: about €125,000–150,000 at 2025 prices.

I looked for countries that already pay to bring startups to the Nordics, but haven't come to TechBBQ yet. That's the shortest path to new revenue.

How I chose them: a government agency already funds pavilions abroad; it already comes to the Nordics (Slush); it's not a TechBBQ partner today; and its startups work in AI, SaaS, climate, health or fintech.
