How I'd grow TechBBQ's international partnerships, starting with the Africa and India corridors. A working plan for the Global Partnerships Manager role.

The corridors work. Now they need partners who pay.

TechBBQ already runs a Nordic–India summit (third edition in 2026) and a Nordic–Africa summit (second edition). Both are carried largely by one three-year Novo Nordisk Foundation grant.

TechBBQ's own Life Science programme shows the next step. It started on the same foundation's money in 2023, and by 2025 the Danish Life Science Cluster was paying DKK 750,000 for an exhibition area of its own. The corridors need that same shift from grant to paying partners, and that is the work of this role.

From fundraising to partnerships.

Fundraising and partnership sales are the same job in two languages. In both, you convince an organisation to invest in your project by showing how it serves their goals. The difference is what they count as a return: a foundation looks for impact, a commercial partner for ROI.

What I do at Crossing BordersWhat it becomes at TechBBQ
Research funders' priorities, evaluation criteria and past grantsResearch prospective partners and qualify the right ones
Write proposals tailored to each funder, such as TrygFonden and Oak FoundationShape sponsorship proposals around each partner's goals
Present budgets, activities and measurable outcomesPresent packages, activations and agreed KPIs
Map potential partners for an international programmeBuild multi-stakeholder partnerships for a country corridor
Plan evaluation and impact reportingDeliver post-event ROI reports that open the renewal conversation

TechBBQ itself works in both worlds. Its Africa and India corridors run on a Novo Nordisk Foundation grant and need to grow through paying partners, so speaking both the foundation and the commercial language is part of the job.

About me.

I'm a partnership and project development professional with a background in event management, stakeholder engagement and strategic project development.

I completed my MBA in Marketing and Strategic Management, focused on multi-stakeholder engagement, and over the past year in Denmark I've been working in international environments, particularly at DTU, where I coordinated student culture and events involving more than 1,000 participants.

More recently, through Crossing Borders, I've been working on partnership development: mapping potential partners, developing funding proposals and identifying opportunities for collaboration.

I've always worked internationally: in Türkiye and now in Denmark, with international artists at festivals, with a multinational student community at DTU, and on an international partnership programme at Crossing Borders.

I approach this work in a research-driven way. For this plan, I worked from TechBBQ's own impact reports and reverse-engineered the partnerships that already work there, to understand what each stakeholder expects and how to meet those expectations. For example, the Life Science programme, funded by a foundation grant, attracted partner co-funding on top of it. I turned those patterns into my own method for growing international partnerships. I also built a KPI Tracker to follow the full project cycle with different stakeholders, from goals and indicators to reporting.

What connects all these experiences is that I enjoy bringing different stakeholders together, understanding their objectives, and turning those into meaningful projects and collaborations. I'm now looking to move further into partnership development in a more commercial and international environment, and that's what particularly attracted me to TechBBQ. For me, partnerships come down to one idea: we're stronger together.
